Arrival

Tens of thousands of migrants come to the Netherlands every year to work

In 2023, that figure was nearly

70.000

Migrant workers

65% of these migrant workers come from the EU

Within the EU, people have the right to live and work freely in other member states. This means they can move to the Netherlands temporarily without having to deal with complicated visas or permits.

This makes it relatively easy for them to find work, gain experience, and participate in the Dutch labor market on a temporary basis. Migrants from Central and Eastern Europe, in particular, take use of this opportunity.

Housing

Housing as long as there is work?

Many migrant workers come to the Netherlands for temporary work. This is reflected in their living arrangements are set up.

65%

of EU migrant workers leave within five years.

Many migrant workers do not know how long their jobs will last. As a result, their housing situation is uncertain.

The workplace often determines where migrant workers live and spend their time.

41%

of migrant workers are employed through temporary staffing agencies, which often means that their work and housing are linked.

No job = no place to live

For many migrant workers, housing is temporary and practical. Privacy and comfort are not a given.

Workplace

Migrant workers are employed in sectors where there is high demand for flexible and physically work

This work is essential but often goes unnoticed. This demand for labor is particularly evident in three sectors.

logistics

In warehouses and distribution centers, migrant workers ensure that goods are sorted, packed, and shipped. Without this work, packages would remain unsent.

agriculture & greenhouses

They grow vegetables, fruit, and flowers in greenhouses and in open fields. This work is seasonal and requires a lot of physical effort.

factories

In factories and production facilities, migrant workers produce the items you see every day at the supermarket, such as bread, meat, and packaged vegetables. They often work behind the scenes, but their work is essential.
